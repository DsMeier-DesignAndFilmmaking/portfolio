**Independent Systems Design Practice · designed June 2026 · founder's view**

## The Founder's Frame (read this first)

A founder with no revenue doesn't launch a catalog. **They launch one thing that bills, get three customers, and let those customers tell them what to build next.** Everything below is sequenced to that reality. The single job for the next 90 days is **sell three Confidence Audits.** The rest of this document exists so you know what those audits turn into — not so you build it all now.

The offerings below are not design services. Each emerges from a specific asset the practice already owns.

## Customer Journey

```
Reads Field Report → runs free Self-Check → buys Confidence Audit
   → converts to Fieldcraft Engagement → becomes case study
   → renews as Advisory Retainer  +  buys Templates  +  subscribes to Field Report Pro
   → refers the next operator
```

Each arrow is a designed handoff, not a hope. The Self-Check ends with "here's your weakest point — an Audit goes deep on it." The Audit ends with "here's the system to fix it — that's a Fieldcraft Engagement." The engagement ends with "here's how to keep it running — that's a retainer."

## Pricing Strategy

1. **Price the wedge low on purpose.** The first 2–3 Audits are "founding-client" priced ($3k) to buy proof and testimonials. Proof is worth more than margin right now.
2. **Value-anchor the engagement.** Price Fieldcraft against the outcome (occupancy, NPS, refund cost), not against hours. Anchor high with the Strategy tier ($25–100k) so the $15–50k engagement reads as reasonable.
3. **Productize for margin, not for cash.** Templates and subscriptions will never be the income — they're authority and lead-gen that happen to earn a little. Don't over-invest in them early.
4. **Recurring is earned, not sold.** Retainers come after an engagement proves value, never as the opening offer.
5. **Sponsored research is the cheat code.** A commissioned study (H) gets a *client to fund the authority asset* you'd otherwise build for free.

## Go-to-Market (the 90-day acquisition play)

This is founder-led direct sales, not a funnel.

- **Target list (build it this week):** 40–50 named outdoor-hospitality operators in the $1–10M band — independent glamping/ranch/eco-lodge groups, plus the new brand-owned units (AutoCamp / Under Canvas / Collective Retreats / Getaway archetype, and the Marriott/Hyatt/Hilton outdoor lines). These have budget *and* the exact problem.
- **The offer:** the Confidence Audit, framed as one sentence — *"I'll find the three places your guests lose confidence, and tell you what to do about it, in three weeks."* No jargon. No "systems design."
- **The motion:** (1) publish **one** outdoor-hospitality confidence teardown as the Field Report's first issue — a public proof-of-thinking; (2) 10 direct founder conversations from the target list; (3) work the warm network for the first intro. No paid ads.
- **The proof goal:** 3 Audits in 90 days → 1 converts to a Fieldcraft Engagement → first named case study → that case study sells the next five.
- **The gate:** if you cannot sell three Audits in 90 days, the problem is the *offer or the buyer*, not the catalog — and no new offering will fix it. Stop and re-frame the wedge before building anything else.

## Bottom Line

You have one move: **publish the first Field Report teardown, put the Confidence Audit in front of 10 operators, and close three.** The service ladder, product ladder, and subscription all exist — but they are *consequences* of those three audits, not prerequisites. The catalog is finished. The selling has not started.
