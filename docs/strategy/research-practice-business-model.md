# Research Practice → The Business Design

**Goal:** Design the business, not the portfolio. Identify revenue streams; map every project to customer, problem, revenue opportunity, business model, market fit, and competitive landscape; and deliver a full commercial strategy.
**Date:** 2026-06-24

---

## The core decision: what business is this?

There are **three** businesses latent in this practice, and trying to run all three at once is the single biggest risk:

| Candidate business | Market | Rates | Competition | Fit with founder |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Horizontal design-strategy consultancy** (Confidence, Recovery) | Large | High | Heavy (every agency) | Strong via frameworks |
| **B. AI decision-governance advisory** (Bounded Authority) | Hot/huge | Highest | Crowded, fast-moving | Weak *brand* today |
| **C. Outdoor/environmental hospitality systems studio** (Adaptive Ranch, Intention, Wayfinding, Responsive Ecologies) | Smaller | Moderate | Light — **ownable** | **Strongest** — matches positioning, corpus, portfolio |

**Recommendation: Lead with C as the beachhead, monetize A as the scalable engine, treat B as a high-margin opportunistic offer — never the core identity.**

C is the only one the founder can *own* today (it matches the 15-year story, the 41-country corpus, and the project portfolio, and faces the least competition). It wins logos and case studies. A (the Architecture of Confidence) is the horizontal engine that lets the business escape the niche's budget ceiling. B is too crowded to bet the brand on but too lucrative to ignore.

**The business model itself: research-led, productized expertise, sold as a ladder** — cheap IP at the bottom (reports, templates, workshops) generates inbound and feeds bespoke advisory at the top. The frameworks and corpus are the marketing; the ladder captures every budget level.

> **What to say NO to (for now):** building software/SaaS (HADE etc. — capital-heavy, unvalidated), subscriptions (no audience yet), and serving all five customer segments simultaneously. A business is defined by what it declines.

## Deliverable 1 — Business Model Canvas

**Customer Segments**
- **Beachhead:** outdoor/environmental hospitality operators (ranches, lodges, eco-resorts, outfitters, adventure parks)
- **Expansion:** destination developers, DMOs, public-land managers (high end of niche)
- **Horizontal:** product/tech teams building high-stakes decision experiences
- **High-margin/opportunistic:** enterprises deploying AI agents
- **Volume/low-touch:** designers, design teams, studios

**Value Propositions**
- Operators: *"Make your guest experience hold up when conditions change — designed across the trail, the app, and the ops behind both."*
- Product teams: *"Design decisions your users actually trust and act on."*
- AI teams: *"Give your AI the right amount of authority — and be able to prove it."*
- Designers: *"Research-grade frameworks you can run yourself."*
- The moat across all: **the integrative, whole-system view** (physical + digital + operations) plus a research-led method competitors can't claim.

**Channels** — Owned: the practice site, framework pages, essays, email list, Field Notes newsletter. Earned: speaking, podcasts, guest writing, referrals. Partner: hospitality networks, design communities, AI/tech firms. Direct outreach for beachhead logos.

**Customer Relationships** — Thought-leadership-led (inbound from frameworks/research); high-touch advisory at the top, self-serve at the bottom; community later.

**Revenue Streams** — Reports + templates (passive) → workshops + training (mid) → consulting (core) → advisory retainers (top). Speaking = marketing. Software/subscriptions/licensing = future options.

**Key Resources** — The frameworks (AOC, Recovery, Bounded Authority, Blueprint); the 41-country corpus; the founder's dual landscape-architecture + digital-systems background (the real moat); the Evidence OS method; the published brand/IP.

**Key Activities** — Publishing/thought leadership (the demand engine); delivering engagements; productizing IP; running pilots → case studies; replenishing field research.

**Key Partners** — Beachhead operators (pilots); other studios (referrals, licensing); academic/research collaborators (fixes the evidence/credibility gap); AI/tech firms (for governance work).

**Cost Structure** — Low and founder-time-dominated (publishing, site, tools, travel). Variable: contractors for builds/production. **The binding constraint is founder time** — so productization is how the business breaks the time-for-money ceiling.

## Deliverable 3 — Service Ladder (high-touch, ascending)

```
0 │ FREE   Essays · frameworks · talks                    -> lead generation
1 │ $2.5-15k   Paid talk / workshop                       -> first paid touch
2 │ $8-25k     Confidence Audit (fixed-scope diagnostic)  -> the entry wedge
3 │ $15-50k    Blueprinting / design sprint               -> expand
4 │ $25-100k+  Full systems-design project                -> flagship engagement
5 │ $3-12k/mo  Advisory retainer (AI-gov: premium)        -> recurring, top margin
```
**Land-and-expand:** the Audit (rung 2) is the wedge that converts into projects (4) and retainers (5).

## Deliverable 4 — Product Ladder (low-touch / passive, ascending)

```
0 │ FREE   Report / newsletter (Field Notes)        -> audience + lead gen
1 │ $29-99    Templates / canvases (Blueprint, Confidence scorecard)
2 │ $149-499  Toolkit / playbook bundle
3 │ $299-999  Self-paced course ("Designing for Confidence")
4 │ $199-999  Evidence OS template / team license
5 │ $499+     Premium research reports / membership
6 │ Custom    Framework licensing + certification        -> IP income beyond time
```
**The two ladders interlock:** free content feeds both; a template buyer becomes an Audit client; a workshop becomes a project. Products monetize the audience the publishing creates; services monetize the high-intent slice of it.

## Deliverable 5 — Customer Segments (detail)

1. **Outdoor/environmental hospitality operators** *(beachhead)* — ranches, lodges, eco-resorts, glamping, outfitters, adventure parks. Pain: experience fails when conditions change; thin ops; guest confidence. Budget: moderate, project-based. Cycle: relationship/referral, slow. **Win these first for case studies.**
2. **Destination developers / DMOs / public-land managers** *(expansion)* — larger, slower, higher-budget projects at the top of the niche.
3. **Product/tech teams (high-stakes decision UX)** *(horizontal scale)* — fintech, health, climate-tech, navigation. Pain: trust, conversion, activation. Budget: higher; cycle: faster. The escape from the niche ceiling.
4. **Enterprises deploying AI agents** *(high-margin, opportunistic)* — pain: agent autonomy/governance. Budget: highest. Crowded; take it when it comes, don't anchor the brand on it.
5. **Designers / teams / studios** *(volume, low-touch)* — buy templates, courses, workshops, licensing. Individually small, collectively a scalable passive layer.

## Deliverable 6 — Pricing Strategy

- **Value-based and fixed-scope, never hourly.** Productize offers (the Audit, the Blueprint) so price reflects outcome, not time. Hourly only for ad-hoc AI-governance advisory where scope is unknowable.
- **Ladder = price discrimination.** Capture the $29 template buyer and the $80k project buyer from the same funnel. Never force a single price point on a varied-budget market.
- **Start lower to win the first 2–3 logos, then move to premium.** With limited validated case studies (evidence audit: RML 2.8), the first beachhead engagements may be discounted or pilot-priced *in exchange for a public case study*. Raise rates 30–50% once proof exists.
- **Niche vs. horizontal pricing.** Hospitality: relationship-priced, longer cycles, retainers for stability. Product/AI: premium, faster, higher anchors.
- **Anchor high, sell the middle.** Publish the full project / retainer tiers so the Audit reads as the easy yes.
- **Bundle IP into services.** Every engagement includes the relevant template/toolkit — raises perceived value and seeds the product ladder.

## Deliverable 7 — Go-To-Market Roadmap

**Phase 0 (0–3 mo) — Foundation & authority** *(prerequisite: the business has no audience or case studies yet)*
- Fix the name/positioning (per positioning audit — one name, principal visible).
- Publish the two flagship essays: **Architecture of Confidence** and **Bounded Authority**.
- Launch the **Field Notes report/newsletter** (turns the corpus into an audience engine).
- Ship 2 templates (**Blueprint canvas**, **Confidence scorecard**) — first dollars + lead capture.
- Define the 3 productized offers (Confidence Audit, Blueprinting, Adaptive-Hospitality engagement) with fixed scope + price.
- *Goal:* credibility, inbound seeds, first low-ticket revenue.

**Phase 1 (3–9 mo) — Beachhead & first cash**
- Land **1–2 outdoor-hospitality pilots** (Adaptive Ranch), pilot-priced for public case studies. *(This is the highest-priority action in the whole plan — it converts the portfolio from hypotheses to proof.)*
- Sell **Confidence Audits** into the warm product/tech network.
- Run the **first paid workshop**.
- *Goal:* 2–3 case studies + first meaningful revenue. Revenue mix: ~80% consulting, ~20% product.

**Phase 2 (9–18 mo) — Scale the ladder**
- Productize the winning engagement into a **repeatable service**; launch the **self-paced course**; add **advisory retainers**.
- Take **opportunistic AI-governance advisory** (highest rates).
- Build the **speaking circuit** (authority + lead gen).
- *Goal:* recurring + mid-ticket revenue; begin breaking the time ceiling. Mix: ~60% consulting, ~25% recurring/retainer, ~15% product.

**Phase 3 (18–36 mo) — Leverage & IP income**
- **Book** (anchored on Architecture of Confidence); **certification** (AOC / Bounded Authority); **framework licensing**.
- Evaluate the **SaaS** (Adaptive Hospitality or HADE) *only if* Phase-1/2 demand validates it.
- *Goal:* scalable IP income independent of founder time. Mix: more balanced across services, recurring, and IP/licensing.

---

## Bottom Line

Design the business as a **research-led, productized-expertise practice**: outdoor/environmental hospitality is the **beachhead** that earns proof, the **Architecture of Confidence** is the horizontal **engine** that escapes the niche, and **AI governance** is the high-margin **opportunistic** play. Monetize through **two interlocking ladders** — passive IP feeding bespoke advisory — so every level of budget and intent converts. The gating moves, in order: **(1) fix positioning + publish the flagship frameworks, (2) turn the corpus into an audience, (3) land one beachhead case study, (4) build the ladder.** Everything else is optionality you fund once those four create demand and proof.
