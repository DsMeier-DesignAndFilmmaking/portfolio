## 1. The Named Methodology — Fieldcraft

**Fieldcraft** — *the confidence-led systems design method.*

Fieldcraft is the skill of operating competently when conditions are real, changing, and unforgiving — exactly what the practice designs *for*, and exactly what it *does*. It is the client-facing name for the internal engine.

> **Naming decision (resolves a red-team finding):** the red-team audit flagged that "Ground Truth" is an established ML/remote-sensing term — un-ownable and SEO-invisible. **Recommendation: retire "Ground Truth" as the market-facing name and adopt Fieldcraft.** The engine underneath is unchanged — the three movements and five disciplines carry over verbatim. "Ground Truth" can survive as an internal engineering label if you're attached to it, but it should not appear in proposals.

**The engine (unchanged): FIELD → MODEL → PROVE**

| Movement | What happens | Discipline enforced |
|---|---|---|
| **FIELD** | Go where the experience actually happens. Observe real decisions, real conditions, real breakdowns. | Traceability |
| **MODEL** | Map the system, locate the decision points, design the confidence + recovery architecture. | Confidence · Whole-system |
| **PROVE** | Pilot it, measure it, hand back evidence — not opinion. | Recovery · Authority |

**The five disciplines (the quality bars that make this not-generic-UX):**
1. **Traceability** — every recommendation traces to a field observation.
2. **Confidence** — designs are judged by whether they measurably increase *confident action*.
3. **Recovery** — every design specifies what happens when it fails.
4. **Authority** — every automation specifies what stays human.
5. **Whole-system** — no point fix that breaks something upstream or downstream.

These five lines are the entire sales differentiator. They go on the website, in the proposal, and at the top of every report.

## 2. Project Phases

| Phase | Purpose | Key activities | Duration | Exit deliverable | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0 · Frame** | De-risk the sale; agree the question | Scoping interview, success metric, access plan | 1 wk (paid) | Engagement Frame (1pg) | Client funds the work? |
| **1 · Field** | See it as it really is | Observation, decision-point interviews, corpus review | 2–4 wks | Field Report + Decision-Point Map | Did we find the real break? |
| **2 · Model** | Design the system | System map, confidence + recovery architecture, framework application | 2–4 wks | System Map + Architecture | Client recognizes themselves? |
| **3 · Prove** | Make it real and measured | Pilot design, measurement, run, read results | 3–6 wks | Pilot + Evidence Brief | Did confidence/metric move? |
| **4 · Embed** | Leave capability behind | Playbook, capability workshop, roadmap | 1–2 wks | Toolkit + Roadmap | Retainer / next engagement? |

Phase 0 is non-negotiable and **paid** — it is the wedge that converts to the full engagement and the red-team-approved "market contact first" discipline in practice.

## 5. Research Methods

The evidence base — what makes the recommendations traceable:

- **Decision-Point Interview** *(signature method)* — elicits the exact moments a person chose to proceed, hesitate, or turn back, and what information they held.
- **Confidence Trace** *(signature method)* — maps a journey by where confidence rose and fell.
- Field / contextual observation in-situ at the operator
- Operator & staff interviews · guest/visitor interviews
- Post-disruption debriefs (recovery incident analysis)
- Corpus analysis (Field Notes) · telemetry analysis where data exists
- Secondary synthesis (the 2026 trend reports)

# The Seven Service Offerings

> **Discipline note:** seven services for a zero-client solo practice is itself a red-team flag. **Lead with two — Outdoor Hospitality and Visitor Experience.** The other five are *named and ready* but should not be marketed until the first two have repeated. The ranking table at the end says which is which.

---

### 1 · Outdoor Hospitality *(the beachhead — lead service)*

- **Problem solved:** Operators (glamping, ranch, eco-lodge) deliver inconsistent, fragile guest experiences as they scale and as conditions vary.
- **Ideal client:** Outdoor-hospitality operator (10–200 units) — and the brands/VC now acquiring them.
- **Deliverables:** Confidence Audit → Adaptive Hospitality Blueprint → pilot + Evidence Brief.
- **Timeline:** 6–10 weeks.
- **Business value:** Higher NPS, occupancy, and rate; de-risks investor/brand scrutiny.
- **Pricing:** $15–40k fixed-scope; Phase 0 audit $5k.
- **Scalability:** High — the Blueprint + Maturity Model productize into a diagnostic.

---

### 2 · Visitor Experience *(lead service)*

- **Problem solved:** Visitors arrive uncertain; experience is inconsistent; low confidence depresses satisfaction and spend.
- **Ideal client:** Attraction, park concessioner, resort experience lead.
- **Deliverables:** Decision-Point Map, Confidence Architecture, pilot.
- **Timeline:** 6–10 weeks.
- **Business value:** Confident visitors stay longer, spend more, return, recommend.
- **Pricing:** $15–35k; audit $2.5–6k.
- **Scalability:** High — Confidence Scorecard is the flagship product.

---

### 3 · Operations *(the retainer engine)*

- **Problem solved:** Experiences break operationally — handoffs fail, recovery is improvised, conditions disrupt delivery.
- **Ideal client:** GM / operations director.
- **Deliverables:** Recovery Playbook, Authority/Delegation Matrix, embedded retainer.
- **Timeline:** 4–6 weeks → ongoing.
- **Business value:** Fewer failures, faster recovery, lower refund/churn cost.
- **Pricing:** $12–30k, then **$3–12k/mo retainer** (the recurring-revenue service).
- **Scalability:** Medium — playbook templatizes; retainer needs the founder early.

---

### 4 · Experience Strategy *(the senior umbrella)*

- **Problem solved:** Org has experiences but no coherent system or strategy connecting them.
- **Ideal client:** Owner / exec / brand at a portfolio operator or destination.
- **Deliverables:** Transformation Blueprint, maturity assessment, multi-year roadmap.
- **Timeline:** 8–14 weeks.
- **Business value:** Aligns investment behind one coherent experience system.
- **Pricing:** $25–100k+ (highest-value, founder-led).
- **Scalability:** Low — bespoke, senior. The margin, not the volume.

---

### 5 · Stewardship *(the fundable / public-interest service)*

- **Problem solved:** Maintaining safety, ecological, and experience quality under collapsing capacity (the parks crisis).
- **Ideal client:** Agencies, gateway communities, concessioners, conservancies, foundations.
- **Deliverables:** Capacity-gap analysis, Bounded Authority model, Recovery + Signal design.
- **Timeline:** 10–16 weeks.
- **Business value:** Does more with less while keeping authority human and defensible.
- **Pricing:** Grant- or cost-plus-scoped ($20–75k); often foundation-funded.
- **Scalability:** Medium — high authority/PR value; slower sales cycle.

---

### 6 · Environmental Systems *(the deep-IP service)*

- **Problem solved:** Managing land/ecology with a mix of data, sensors, and human judgment — and not knowing what to trust.
- **Ideal client:** Land managers, outfitters, eco-lodges, conservancies.
- **Deliverables:** Authority/Delegation Matrix, confidence-gated decision model, stewardship playbook.
- **Timeline:** 8–14 weeks.
- **Business value:** Safer, evidence-based stewardship decisions; AI used responsibly.
- **Pricing:** $20–60k.
- **Scalability:** Low–medium — this is where the most original IP matures; keep it founder-led until validated.

---

### 7 · Destination Development *(the expansion / big-ticket service)*

- **Problem solved:** A place wants to grow as a destination without overtourism — managing dispersal, capacity, and competing stakeholder authority.
- **Ideal client:** DMOs, destination developers, public-private partnerships.
- **Deliverables:** Signal Matrix (demand-shaping), multi-stakeholder Authority model, dispersal design.
- **Timeline:** 12–20 weeks.
- **Business value:** Growth without resentment or ecological damage; defensible governance.
- **Pricing:** $40–100k+.
- **Scalability:** Low — large, complex, relationship-driven. The 2027–28 expansion bet.

# How Engagements Feed Research and Products

Every engagement is a funded field study — consulting, research, and products are one system, not three.

| Service engagement | Feeds research study | Seeds product |
|---|---|---|
| Outdoor Hospitality | Study 1 (Confidence) + Study 5 (Standard) | Confidence Scorecard · Hospitality Blueprint diagnostic |
| Visitor Experience | Study 1 (Confidence) | Confidence Scorecard |
| Operations | Study 3 (Climate Recovery) | Recovery Playbook toolkit |
| Stewardship | Study 2 (Capacity Gap) | Bounded Authority toolkit · the public-lands report |
| Environmental Systems | Study 4 (Regenerative/Authority) | Authority/Delegation Matrix |
| Destination Development | Study 6 (Dispersal) | Signal Matrix product |

**The loop:**
1. An engagement is delivered *and* run as a field study (work-emits-research).
2. It moves a framework up the maturity ladder (RML climb) and produces a **case study** (authority).
3. The repeated deliverable hardens into a **product** (toolkit, diagnostic, template).
4. The product + case study generate inbound → the next engagement. Flywheel turns.

**Rule (red-team discipline):** productize a deliverable **only after the third paid repeat**, never before. Fieldcraft is a hypothesis until Engagement #1 proves it.
