# Practice Research Agenda

*The whole portfolio, read as one long-term research program — organized by intellectual theme.*

> **What this document is, and is not.** Three organizing axes already exist and are not repeated here:
> - **Delivery/market grouping of every project** → `practice-taxonomy-audit.md` (8 Practice Areas + full project classification + consulting/product table).
> - **Market-timed proof studies**, each with commercial, publication, and speaking angles → `research-practice-research-program.md` (the 6 Studies).
> - **Channels, pillars, framework papers, and the speaking ladder** → `research-practice-thought-leadership.md`; concept dispositions (fund/harvest/park/extract) → `research-practice-concept-portfolio.md`.
>
> This adds the missing fourth axis: the enduring **research themes** the practice investigates, how *every* project — research and client — feeds them, and where the white space is. For the operational detail of commercial, publications, and speaking, each theme points to the doc that owns it rather than restating it.

**How to read this.** A theme is an enduring question the practice keeps investigating, not a service or a project bucket. A single project feeds several themes. Client work counts — here it is read as *evidence*, not just delivery.

**The cross-cutting spine.** Three commitments run through every theme — **decision confidence**, **human agency**, and **traceable trust**. They are the manifesto's convictions and Principles 6–8, 10–11 (`practice-design-principles.md`). They are why these themes cohere into one program rather than ten unrelated interests. Two of them (Human Agency, Trust) are also strong enough to carry their own projects, so they appear below both as spine *and* as themes.

## Core inquiry

### 1 · Decision Systems & Confidence

**Why it matters.** This is the practice's center of gravity: how people make go/no-go and next-step decisions as conditions change, and where information increases *confident action* versus anxiety. The 2026 market is independently demanding it ("decision-detox," seamless ultraluxe).

- **Current projects:** Architecture of Confidence (flagship framework) · CATDS (context-aware decision logic) · Spontaneity Engine · HADE (adaptive decision support) · Digital Executor (recovery decisions).
- **White space:** a **pure-digital confidence product** (the portfolio's #1 gating gap — proves the framework transfers beyond the outdoors); a validated Confidence Scorecard and decision-point taxonomy; an instrument that *measures* confident action.
- **Five-year direction:** move Architecture of Confidence from concept to evidenced, then generalize into a theory of confidence under variable conditions across domains → `research-program` **Study 1 (flagship)**.
- **Commercial · Publications · Speaking:** Confidence Audit + recovery workshop → see taxonomy consulting table · the Confidence pillar + AoC framework paper → thought-leadership Q1 · "confident decisions under uncertainty" at ATTA / glamping stages.

### 2 · Environmental Intelligence

**Why it matters.** Treating weather, terrain, season, and ecological load as first-class inputs is the line between this practice and screen-only UX. It is the technical spine of "we design for the day conditions change" (Principle 5).

- **Current projects:** Responsive Ecologies (multi-signal stewardship) · Environmental Systems Design OS (the research substrate) · Wayfinding Matrix (signal → route readiness) · CATDS (environmental sensing) · *client proof:* Intel (sustainability systems).
- **White space:** a validated **Signal Matrix** (signal → decision), currently conceptual; a **low-sensor / low-cost** version that escapes the hardware-and-liability trap; an urban-ecology transfer.
- **Five-year direction:** validate the Signal Matrix and Bounded Authority through stewardship work; open the urban-ecology transfer as Horizon 2 → `research-program` **Studies 2, 4, 6**.
- **Commercial · Publications · Speaking:** stewardship-systems & climate-resilience advisory → framework-commercialization · "capacity collapse on public lands" (the most cite-able pillar) → thought-leadership Q3 · George Wright Society.

### 3 · Wayfinding & Orientation

**Why it matters.** Keeping a person certain of where they are, what is happening, and what to do next — across physical and digital space, most of all when conditions degrade. It is the most direct expression of "confidence before efficiency" (Principle 6), and the clearest place the landscape-architecture-to-digital through-line becomes a framework.

- **Current projects:** Wayfinding Matrix (flagship) · Context-Aware Detours (path decisions) · *client proof, read as evidence:* Purdue (web/CMS IA) · Rich Products (nav/content IA) · Healthcare (IA + accessibility) · TimberTech (commerce IA).
- **White space:** a **non-screen / ambient** wayfinding pilot (Matrix Phase 4, needs an operator); a **unified physical-digital orientation model** that bridges the client IA body of work and the outdoor wayfinding concepts — nothing yet connects them.
- **Five-year direction:** a general orientation model spanning digital information architecture and physical wayfinding — arguably the practice's most defensible original framework, because the client work already proves half of it.
- **Commercial · Publications · Speaking:** wayfinding & safety-signal audits → taxonomy · an orientation framework paper (secondary pillar) · parks + service-design stages.

## Domain programs

### 4 · Adaptive Hospitality

**Why it matters.** The beachhead: guest experience that reads conditions and adapts rather than running a script. It is the funded market (major-brand and VC entry) and the nearest recurring revenue.

- **Current projects:** Adaptive Outdoor Hospitality Companion (flagship, most operationally believable) · Intention Engine (guest intent → experience) · *client proof:* McDonald's Kiosk (adaptive service/upsell) · DoubleGood (mobile experience).
- **White space:** a validated **Adaptive Hospitality Blueprint** (via a real operator pilot); an experience-maturity model; a guest-companion + operations product (deferred until the pilot proves it).
- **Five-year direction:** establish the Outdoor-Hospitality Experience Standard and the annual "State of…" report → `research-program` **Studies 1 + 5** (`concept-portfolio`: **FUND** this one first).
- **Commercial · Publications · Speaking:** the beachhead engagement + benchmarking diagnostic (nearest revenue) · the annual **flagship report** → thought-leadership Q4 · Outdoor Hospitality Conference & Expo, The Glamping Show.

### 5 · Landscape & Stewardship Systems

**Why it matters.** Land, ecology, carrying capacity, and regenerative operations — designing *with* environments, not on top of them (Principle 3). It sits on the 2026 regenerative frontier and the public-lands capacity emergency, and it holds the practice's most original IP.

- **Current projects:** Responsive Ecologies (stewardship platform / Bounded Authority) · Environmental Systems Design OS (land-operations research) · *client proof:* Intel (sustainability).
- **White space:** a **validated, extracted Bounded Authority** framework (today it is buried inside Responsive Ecologies — `concept-portfolio` says **EXTRACT** it); a carrying-capacity / dispersal model; a regenerative-operations standard.
- **Five-year direction:** the Stewardship Capacity Gap and Regenerative/Bounded-Authority studies, then humane visitor dispersal → `research-program` **Studies 2, 4, 6**.
- **Commercial · Publications · Speaking:** stewardship decision-governance consulting + **grant-funded** research (Study 2 is the fundable one) · "how much authority should you give the system?" + the public-lands report · George Wright Society + conservation stages (the international-attention play).

### 6 · Experience & Narrative Systems

**Why it matters.** Designing meaning, intention, and how a place *feels* over time — experience as a composed narrative rather than a transaction. The practice's most editorial and emotional territory.

- **Current projects:** Intention Engine (intention → experience) · Narrative Architecture (place as story over time) · Narrative Experience Generator (narrative surface) · *client proof:* Havas (experience/campaign work).
- **White space:** a **field-validated intention taxonomy** grounded in real guest language — the `evidence-audit` flags the current taxonomy as the portfolio's highest source-credibility risk; a repeatable narrative-experience method.
- **Five-year direction:** validate the intention/experience method through guest-language studies run inside hospitality engagements; a general "experience over time" framework.
- **Commercial · Publications · Speaking:** destination positioning + the Blueprint as a billable deliverable (`concept-portfolio`: **HARVEST**) · an experience-design pillar (secondary) · service-design + destination stages (year 2).

### 7 · Relational & Social Systems

**Why it matters.** How a system decides that a human connection is relevant enough to surface, and at what privacy cost. Honestly peripheral to the environmental core and the least validated cluster — the practice's most speculative territory.

- **Current projects:** Social Capital Orchestrator · Relational Heuristics Engine · Social Opportunity Matching · Privacy-Preserving Social Graph.
- **White space:** *any* field grounding — all four are architected, none tested; a version that is not privacy-fraught at scale.
- **Five-year direction:** mostly **PARK** (`concept-portfolio`) — advance only opportunistically; the one defensible research line is *relevance and consent in social surfacing*.
- **Commercial · Publications · Speaking:** low near-term; nothing published until validated. Held as an option, not a program.

## The cross-cutting spine, surfaced as themes

### 8 · Trust, Transparency & Provenance

**Why it matters.** Principles 10–11 as a research theme: traceable recommendations, verifiable sourcing, overrulable systems. It cross-cuts every AI-touching project and is the precondition for trusting any adaptive system at all.

- **Current projects:** Trust Framework for AI Travel (flagship) · Privacy-Preserving Social Graph · Relational Heuristics (verification) · plus a *required layer* inside HADE, CATDS, and Responsive Ecologies.
- **White space:** a **portable provenance/traceability model** usable across domains — the highest-value, most-timely white space in the portfolio (`concept-portfolio`: the AI-governance slice to **EXTRACT** and stake now); a validated trust-verification instrument.
- **Five-year direction:** give the AI-governance claim a credible, owned origin in stewardship, then generalize a traceability standard → `research-program` **Study 4**.
- **Commercial · Publications · Speaking:** trust/verification strategy + the opportunistic AI-governance market · a provenance pillar (shares the Bounded Authority paper with Theme 5) · AI + travel-trust stages (year 2+).

### 9 · Human Agency

**Why it matters.** Principle 8 as a standing research question: how systems support judgment without eroding authorship, especially under stress. Not a project cluster — the commitment that threads through every theme and most distinguishes this practice from automation-first AI.

- **Current projects:** cross-cutting — Bounded Authority (Responsive Ecologies), the human-in-the-loop stance of HADE / CATDS / Digital Executor, and "keep the operator in authority" across Wayfinding and Stewardship.
- **White space:** a way to **measure agency preservation** — an agency scorecard; empirical study of *where more information or automation erodes rather than supports agency* (a question `research-program` Study 1 already poses). Today this is a principle with no instrument.
- **Five-year direction:** an empirical theory *and* instrument for agency-preserving decision support — potentially the practice's most original scholarly contribution.
- **Commercial · Publications · Speaking:** a differentiator baked into every engagement (not sold alone) · "when help becomes harm: support that preserves agency" (the intellectual pillar) · the recognition keynote (year 3 — the lagging international-attention play).

## The meta-theme

### 10 · Research Infrastructure & Framework Design

**Why it matters.** How the practice turns field observation into reusable, citable frameworks — the "Ground Truth" method itself. The meta-theme that makes the other nine possible, resting on the practice's deepest moat: the 41-country field corpus.

- **Current projects:** Environmental Systems Design OS (the research substrate) · Field Notes (field capture) · the Ground Truth method.
- **White space:** the **documented Field Notes protocol + coded corpus** — the one honest pre-revenue research task (`research-program`); until it exists, "41 countries" stays out of research claims. Also: a public framework library.
- **Five-year direction:** mature the corpus into a citable evidence base and publish an account of the method.
- **Commercial · Publications · Speaking:** research-system / practice-OS consulting + the method as a template product · a methodology paper + the framework library as the compounding moat → thought-leadership · research-method and practice-building talks (year 2+).

## What the whole agenda says

Read as one program, three things become clear:

1. **The center of gravity is decision confidence in environmental and hospitality systems** (Themes 1–5). Everything else is either an application of it (6–7), a precondition for it (8–9), or the machinery that produces it (10). The practice is not ten interests; it is one thesis investigated from ten angles.
2. **The white space is consistent across themes, and it is not "more concepts."** It is *proof and portability*: a validated framework, a pure-digital transfer, an agency instrument, a documented corpus. The portfolio is rich in architected hypotheses and poor in field validation — the same finding the `concept-portfolio` reached. The missing projects that matter are the ones that *validate*, not the ones that add.
3. **This agenda does not license new studies.** It prioritizes existing ones. The binding discipline stays the `research-program`'s: research runs *through* paid work, every study emits a framework + a publication + a sellable instrument, and no unfunded field studies happen before the first engagement. A theme map makes the program legible; it does not exempt it from the red-team rule.

The single highest-leverage move the agenda points to is unchanged: **push one theme (Adaptive Hospitality) to a real pilot**, and let that one validated case study become the proof, the publication, and the speaking credibility that every other theme is currently waiting on.
