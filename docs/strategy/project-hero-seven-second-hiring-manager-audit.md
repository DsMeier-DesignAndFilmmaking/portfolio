# Project Hero Seven-Second Hiring Manager Audit

Review lens: first viewport only, as a hiring manager with seven seconds.

Goal: determine whether each hero communicates immediate value without scrolling.

Seven-second questions:

1. What is this?
2. Why does it matter?
3. Why is it unique?
4. Why should I continue reading?

This document provides recommendations only. It does not change page architecture or recommend implementation details.

## Scoring Key

- **Clarity:** Can I tell what the project is?
- **Confidence:** Does the page feel assured and credible?
- **Professionalism:** Does it feel suitable for senior/client-facing review?
- **Editorial quality:** Does the first viewport feel composed and well-edited?
- **Innovation:** Does the unique idea come through quickly?
- **Visual simplicity:** Does the visual help rather than slow comprehension?
- **Overall first impression:** Would I keep reading?

### Common Hiring-Manager Reactions

- "This person thinks in systems" comes through strongly.
- "I need to slow down to understand this" happens too often.
- Client work communicates credibility faster than research work.
- Research work communicates originality, but sometimes at the cost of immediate clarity.
- The strongest pages feel edited; the weakest pages feel like they are trying to prove everything at once.

## Ideal First Viewport By Project Type

### Research / Framework Projects

Ideal first viewport:

1. Small eyebrow.
2. Project title.
3. One-sentence abstract.
4. Optional credibility/status line.
5. Quiet architecture preview or no visual.

Seven-second answer:

- What is this? A framework or research system.
- Why does it matter? It helps make complex decisions clearer.
- Why is it unique? It turns patterns into a reusable method.
- Why continue? To see the model and evidence.

### Experience / Concept Projects

Ideal first viewport:

1. Editorial title.
2. One calm positioning sentence.
3. Atmospheric visual or simplified concept visual.
4. No tags, dense labels, or figcaptions.

Seven-second answer:

- What is this? A concept or experience system.
- Why does it matter? It improves how people navigate, recover, decide, or experience place.
- Why is it unique? It combines systems thinking with human context.
- Why continue? To see how the concept works.

### Client Proof Projects

Ideal first viewport:

1. Full-width client/product visual.
2. Client/project title.
3. One proof sentence.
4. No metrics until below the fold.

Seven-second answer:

- What is this? A shipped or professional client project.
- Why does it matter? It proves real-world delivery.
- Why is it unique? The specific domain, role, or artifact.
- Why continue? To see the work and outcomes.

### Travel + AI Logic Projects

Ideal first viewport:

1. Short technical title.
2. Plain-language value sentence.
3. One restrained visual preview.
4. No Challenge/Solution/Core Pillars.
5. No scenario metadata.

Seven-second answer:

- What is this? A travel intelligence system or module.
- Why does it matter? It helps people act under changing conditions.
- Why is it unique? It translates live context into useful guidance.
- Why continue? To see the logic and prototype.

## Final Recommendation

The portfolio already communicates intelligence, ambition, and originality. The opportunity is to make those qualities legible faster.

The ideal first viewport should not try to win the case. It should earn the next scroll.

For a hiring manager, the strongest first viewport is:

1. Clear project identity.
2. One specific value sentence.
3. One restrained proof signal.
4. No dense explanation.

If the hiring manager can answer "what is this?" and "why should I keep reading?" within seven seconds, the hero is doing its job.
