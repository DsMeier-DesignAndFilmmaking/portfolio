# Property Framing Analysis

**Purpose:** Resolve Blocker 2 (Whitetail Club vs. Shore Lodge framing) from `01-system-planning.md` §11 Q2.
**Companion to:** [source-register.md](source-register.md) — all `[P*]` / `[S*]` IDs below are defined there.
**Scope:** Strategic framing only. No product redesign, no new research.

---

## 1 — What is explicitly supported

Six findings, each traceable to a source in the register.

### 1.1 One legal entity holds the binding resource right — **strongest available evidence**

Water Right No. 78-12476 is held by **"Shore Lodge Whitetail LLC"** `[P1]`, `[S10]`, `[S20]`. A single legal person holds the 1.78 CFS diversion limit and the 89-acre irrigation cap that governs the entire managed landscape.

**This is the decisive fact of the framing question.** Whatever the two names mean in marketing, the *stewardship-relevant* constraint — water — is held once, spent once, and cannot be divided between them. Two properties competing for one legal ceiling is a systems relationship, not a coincidence of branding.

### 1.2 The research corpus consistently treats them as a paired, single-managed system

Across the 26 corpus documents, the phrasing is stable: **"Whitetail Club and Shore Lodge"** (11 occurrences), never one as a subsidiary of the other. `[S2]` maps them as **"the Whitetail/Shore Lodge system"** — explicitly one system. Titles bind them: `[S2]`, `[S3]`, `[S4]`.

### 1.3 Shore Lodge appears as a *facility* within a shared physical inventory

`[S2]` inventories physical components as *"Forests, golf course (Dominator Bentgrass), Payette Lake water source, roads/paths, facilities (**Shore Lodge, Clubhouse**), and heavy equipment."*

Shore Lodge is enumerated alongside the Clubhouse as a building within one operational estate — not as a separate estate.

### 1.4 One operational headquarters

`[S1]` places the operational hub at **501 West Lake Street, McCall, Idaho 83638** — a single grounds/maintenance command point, with no evidence of a parallel organization.

### 1.5 One management hierarchy spans both

`[S5]`, `[S6]` describe a single chain — VP of Maintenance & Operations → Grounds Director → Superintendent → Assistant Superintendent → crew — with responsibility explicitly spanning **"Golf, Parks, and Janitorial" teams operating "under one roof"** `[S5]`. Janitorial and Parks are hospitality-side functions. One org, both domains.

### 1.6 The shared environmental envelope is total

Watershed `[S3]`, `[S8]`; wildfire exposure and buffer role `[P4]`, `[S7]`; snow load and Nov–Apr snowpack `[S8]`, `[S9]`; 50-foot shoreline setbacks `[S3]`; IDEQ exposure `[S3]`. **Not one environmental condition in the entire corpus applies to one name and not the other.** They occupy the same basin, the same slope, the same weather, the same regulator.

## 2 — What is assumed

Recorded honestly, because these gaps constrain how confidently any model can be recommended.

| # | Assumption in play | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **A1** | That Whitetail Club and Shore Lodge are **two distinguishable operational domains** (private club/golf/residential vs. lakefront hotel/hospitality). | **Inferred, not sourced.** Strongly implied by the naming and by `[S2]`'s facility inventory, but **no corpus document defines either property's boundary or program.** This is the single largest gap in the framing evidence. |
| **A2** | That the two share crews and equipment rather than running parallel teams. | **Inferred** from the single hierarchy `[S5]` and single HQ `[S1]`. Not stated directly. |
| **A3** | That "Whitetail Shore Lodge" (the current case-study title) names a real thing. | **False.** This construction appears in **no source** — not in the corpus, not in the legal entity name. It is an artifact of the handoff. |
| **A4** | That the handoff's combined framing was a deliberate analytical decision. | **Unsupported.** The handoff asserts *"Properties: Whitetail Club & Shore Lodge"* in its header and never revisits it. The plural noun is the only trace of a framing decision anywhere in v2. |
| **A5** | That research coverage is balanced across the two. | **False — and material.** See §3. |

### The asymmetry that matters most

The corpus is **overwhelmingly about the Whitetail Club grounds**: golf agronomy, irrigation, turf, trails, forest buffers, fleet. Shore Lodge appears substantively **twice** — once as a facility in an inventory `[S2]`, once as a named **data gap**: *"Granular data on non-golf property infrastructure (e.g., Shore Lodge HVAC/janitorial logs)"* is listed among missing information.

**The research does not currently support a Shore Lodge hospitality-operations story.** Any model that promises one is writing a cheque the evidence cannot cash. This constrains the recommendation more than any other single finding.

## 3 — Evaluation of the three models

### Model A — One Property · One Experience · One System

**Strengths**
- Simplest narrative; one place, one story.
- Consistent with the single legal entity `[P1]` and single hierarchy `[S5]`.
- Matches the current handoff without rework.

**Weaknesses**
- **Requires asserting A1 is false** — that the two names denote one property. No evidence supports that, and the corpus's consistent "X and Y" phrasing works against it.
- Perpetuates the fictional "Whitetail Shore Lodge" construction (A3).
- **Flattens the most interesting systems property in the entire project:** two functionally different landscapes competing for one legal water ceiling. Model A deletes the tension rather than designing for it.
- A design director familiar with the region would recognize the conflation immediately, and the error would undermine every other claim in the case study.

**Verdict:** Rejected. Simplicity purchased with a factual error is not simplicity.

### Model B — Two Properties · Two Experiences · One Shared Stewardship System

**Strengths**
- **Matches every piece of §1 evidence without straining any of it.** One entity, one hierarchy, one HQ, one environmental envelope, two named places.
- Makes the shared resource ceiling — 1.78 CFS across both `[P1]` — a *designed* systems relationship rather than an inconvenience.
- Explains why one stewardship system is the right scope, instead of assuming it.
- Preserves the honest distinction between a golf/residential landscape and a lakefront hospitality property.
- Absorbs the coverage asymmetry gracefully: the system spans both; the demonstration goes deep where evidence is deep.

**Weaknesses**
- Requires A1, which is inferred rather than sourced.
- **"Two Experiences" is a genuine hazard** — read literally it doubles the product surface. See the refinement in §4.
- Slightly more narrative setup: the reader must hold two places before the system resolves them.

**Verdict:** **Recommended.**

### Model C — Two Separate Case Studies

**Strengths**
- Cleanest separation of evidence.
- Aligns with existing practice roadmap documents, which list "Shore Lodge" and "Whitetail Club" as two future case studies.
- Would let each be positioned independently.

**Weaknesses**
- **Directly contradicted by `[P1]`.** You cannot write two independent stewardship case studies about one water right. The binding constraint is shared; splitting it misrepresents both.
- **A Shore Lodge case study has almost no evidence** — the corpus names Shore Lodge operations as a *gap*, not a subject. It would require new primary research the project has been told not to conduct.
- Splits a single management hierarchy `[S5]` across two narratives.
- **Portfolio cost:** the practice already carries Rock Creek OS and the Adaptive Ranch Companion in adjacent territory. Adding two more ranch/resort stewardship pieces makes four, and adjacency collapse (`01-system-planning.md` R6) becomes near-certain.

**Verdict:** Rejected on evidence and on portfolio strategy alike.

## 4 — Recommendation

> **Adopt Model B — with one structural refinement: two operational *domains*, not two product experiences.**

**Two domains. One system. One set of experiences.**

The four core experiences defined in `01-system-planning.md` §8 are organized by **posture** (field vs. management), not by property. That must not change. Model B's "two experiences" is read here as **two operational domains within one product** — the same four experiences, applied across two landscapes with different programs and different stewardship regimes.

Read the other way, Model B would duplicate the product per property, doubling scope and violating the project's own complexity constraint. **The domains are two; the system, the record, and the experiences remain one.**

**Grounded in:**

- **Evidence** — every finding in §1 supports one operating entity spanning two named places; nothing supports either full separation or full merger.
- **Systems-design coherence** — one shared spatial record with two domain contexts is precisely the structure that the shared 1.78 CFS ceiling `[P1]` demands. Two landscapes drawing on one legal water limit **is the systems relationship**, and it is materially more interesting than the handoff's original framing.
- **Portfolio quality** — differentiates from Rock Creek OS and the Adaptive Ranch Companion, avoids a fourth adjacent case study, and lets the case study demonstrate depth where evidence is genuinely deep.
- **Storytelling clarity** — "two landscapes, one water right, one crew, one memory" is a cleaner and more compelling premise than either "one big resort" or two disconnected studies.

|---|---|
| **Retire** | "Whitetail Shore Lodge" — appears in no source (A3) |
| **Adopt** | **"Whitetail Club & Shore Lodge"** — the corpus's own consistent usage `[S2]`, `[S3]`, `[S4]` |
| **Note where relevant** | Legal entity of record: **Shore Lodge Whitetail LLC** `[P1]` |

## 5 — Model B definition

### 5.1 Shared geography

One basin, one slope, one weather system, one regulator.

| Condition | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Payette Lake watershed — shared water source and shared receiving water for all runoff | `[S3]`, `[S8]` |
| 50-foot shoreline setbacks; mandatory stormwater BMPs; IDEQ exposure | `[S3]`, `[P2]` |
| Wildfire exposure, Aug–early Oct, west/southwest wind-driven; managed landscape as community fire barrier | `[P4]`, `[S7]` |
| Snowpack Nov–Apr; 12-ft snow storage easements; 20-ft emergency access; roof-shedding geometry | `[S8]`, `[S9]`, `[P2]` |
| Granitic/sandy nutrient-poor soils; late frosts; short growing season; knapweed pressure in disturbed soil | `[S7]`, `[S8]` |
| Steep terrain (slopes >35%) and elevation microsites | `[S8]` |

**No environmental condition in the corpus distinguishes the two.** Weather does not read property lines.

### 5.2 Shared stewardship concerns

| Concern | Why it spans both | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **The 1.78 CFS diversion ceiling and 89-acre irrigation cap** | One legal right, one entity, zero-sum. Every irrigated acre on one side is unavailable to the other. **The strongest shared constraint in the project.** | `[P1]`, `[S1]`, `[S10]` |
| **One workforce and one management hierarchy** | "Golf, Parks, and Janitorial… under one roof" | `[S5]` |
| **One equipment fleet** | Shared leased fleet, shared mechanic, shared 600-hr ceilings | `[S2]`, `[P7]` |
| **Institutional memory loss** | One crew, one turnover problem, one memory. **The case study's thesis is shared by definition.** | `[S2]`, `[S4]`, `[S7]` |
| **Seasonal transition** | One snow regime, one winterization window, one fiscal calendar | `[S9]` |
| **Watershed and regulatory compliance** | Shared receiving water; a violation anywhere is a violation for the entity | `[S3]` |
| **Wildfire buffer obligation** | The managed landscape protects the wider community, not a parcel | `[P4]`, `[S7]` |

### 5.3 Distinct experience domains

**Stated at the confidence the evidence actually supports** — this is inference (A1), and the Shore Lodge column is thin by acknowledgment, not by omission.

| | **Whitetail Club domain** | **Shore Lodge domain** |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary program** | Private club: championship golf, residential/PUD landscape, forest and trail buffer | Lakefront hospitality: guest-facing grounds, shoreline frontage, building services |
| **Dominant stewardship mode** | Agronomic — turf biology, irrigation precision, forest fuel management | Facility and shoreline — guest safety, snow shedding, waterfront condition |
| **Time signature** | Biological windows: frost delay, snow mold's 48-hour window, blowout "point of no return" `[S9]` | Guest-occupancy cycles; continuous year-round operation |
| **Failure mode** | Turf loss, water-right non-compliance, fire-buffer degradation | Guest injury (ice shedding, walkway condition), shoreline degradation, service interruption |
| **Evidence depth** | **Deep** — the corpus's overwhelming focus | **Thin** — named as a data gap `[S17]` |

> **Publication constraint.** The Shore Lodge column is currently supported by inference plus one facility mention. The case study **may scope the system across both domains** — the shared constraints in §5.2 justify that — but **must not present detailed Shore Lodge operational workflows as evidenced.** Depth belongs where the research is. Say so plainly; a stated boundary reads as rigor.

### 5.4 Why one stewardship system logically spans both

Five reasons, in descending strength:

1. **One water right, zero-sum.** 1.78 CFS and 89 acres are held once `[P1]`. A system that manages irrigation for one domain while blind to the other cannot manage the constraint that actually binds. **This alone requires a shared system.**
2. **One crew, one memory.** The turnover that erases spatial knowledge erases it across both domains simultaneously — same people, same hierarchy `[S5]`, same gap.
3. **One environmental envelope.** A thaw, a heat stretch, a fire wind, a snow load arrives at both at once. Splitting the record would mean modeling one weather event twice.
4. **One regulatory exposure.** IDEQ, shoreline setbacks, and ACSP compliance attach to the entity, not the parcel `[S3]`, `[P3]`.
5. **One physical continuity.** Shoreline, forest buffer, and drainage run through both. There is no line at which a culvert stops being a shared concern.

**The one-sentence version, for the case study:**

> *Two landscapes with different programs, one legal water ceiling, one crew, and one memory — which is exactly why they need one system and not two.*

## 6 — Framing risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **A1 is inferred, not sourced.** The domain distinction rests on naming and one facility mention. | State it as a stated framing assumption in the handoff's evidence boundary. Resolving it requires only a property-boundary/program source — a cheap fix. |
| **Shore Lodge depth is promised but not evidenced.** | Enforce the §5.3 publication constraint. Scope across both; demonstrate where evidence is deep; say which is which. |
| **"Two domains" quietly becomes "two products."** | Hold the §4 refinement: four experiences, one record, two domain contexts. Revisit if a fifth experience ever appears. |
| **Roadmap conflict.** Practice documents list Shore Lodge and Whitetail Club as two separate future case studies; Model B consumes both. | Deliberate. Model C is rejected on evidence *and* on adjacency-collapse grounds. Those roadmap entries should be updated to one entry — a net reduction in a portfolio already crowded in this territory. |
| **The shared-signal-registry test.** `environmental-systems-design-platform.md` §5 proposed proving the registry by having Shore Lodge and Whitetail share signals — which presumed two case studies. | **The test survives in better form.** Signal-sharing across two *domains* inside one case study is a cleaner demonstration than sharing across two case studies, and it can be shown rather than asserted. |
