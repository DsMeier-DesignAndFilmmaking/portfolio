## 1. Case Study Thesis

### One-Sentence Thesis *(unchanged from v2)*
To defend the environmental and brand equity of premium mountain developments, luxury resorts must bridge the physical and informational legibility gap between a highly dynamic, climate-stressed terrain and a transient, geographically isolated workforce by translating spatial geography into a shared digital representation and decision layer.

### Core Design Question *(unchanged from v2)*
*How can digital product design make a complex, changing physical landscape more legible, actionable, and knowable to the people responsible for stewarding it?*

### Central Systemic Problem *(revised v3.1 — thesis realigned per governance §1)*
Place-based operational knowledge of a large, complex mountain landscape does not survive the property's seasonal-workforce model `[SRC]`. Knowledge of where distributed and subsurface assets sit, how individual micro-zones behave, and why past stewardship choices were made is held individually and lost on departure `[SRC]` — and buried-utility as-built documentation is explicitly incomplete `[SRC]`. Without a system to capture and transfer spatial knowledge, land operations remain reactive and dependent on individual memory, shortening physical asset lifecycles and leaving the landscape harder to read each time the crew turns over. This friction is amplified by a compressed, volatile seasonal calendar in which several stewardship decisions are irreversible `[SRC]`, and it plays out under real environmental and regulatory constraints — snowpack, fire season, watershed obligations, and a fixed irrigation water right `[SRC]` — which shape the operating context without being the product's subject.

> **What changed in v3.1 and why.** v3 promoted the 1.78 CFS water right to the center of the problem statement. It was the strongest-sourced fact available, and that is precisely why it drifted there — see governance §1 Gravity Test and §9 E5. The water right is a **condition this landscape operates under**, on the same footing as snowpack and frost windows. It is not what the product is about. The spine is restored to spatial knowledge, legibility, and institutional memory — the project's actual thesis and, independently, its best-evidenced problem.

### Portfolio Positioning Statement *(revised — acreage removed)*
At the intersection of Landscape Architecture, Systems Design, and Digital Product Design, this case study outlines a unified digital representation layer for a mountain resort to transition from reactive maintenance to proactive stewardship. It maps spatial terrain, coordinates seasonal changes, and supports contextual decision-making across two operational domains — a championship golf and residential landscape, and a lakefront hospitality property — that share one landscape, one seasonal calendar, and one institutional-knowledge problem `[SRC]`.

## 2. Three Core Problem Domains

### Problem Domain A: The Spatial Knowledge Transfer Bottleneck

*   **The Problem:** *(unchanged)* The loss of place-based, subsurface, and operational asset layout memory due to the continuous turnover of seasonal grounds crews.
*   **Source Evidence:** *(re-sourced)* The property spans multiple subdivisions, an 18-hole championship course, a 10-hole par-3 course on 35 acres, forest buffer, and lake frontage `[S1]`, requiring specialized turf `[S2]`, forest `[S7]`, and shoreline care `[S3]` — yet institutional continuity depends on individually-held knowledge `[S2]`, `[S4]`, and **as-built documentation for buried utilities is explicitly incomplete** `[S1]`, `[S4]`.
*   **Environmental Dimension:** *(unchanged)* Neglected micro-zones, unmapped trail drainage errors, and poor plant care leading to soil erosion.
*   **Operational Dimension:** *(unchanged)* Inability of temporary crews to locate distributed assets (irrigation valves, culverts), leading to high training costs and reactive maintenance delays.
*   **Human Dimension:** *(unchanged)* High cognitive load on seasonal crews entering a massive, unfamiliar landscape with a high-stakes expectation of pristine visual luxury.
*   **Information Gap:** *(unchanged)* No centralized, mobile-accessible directory of geodistributed assets. **Now directly corroborated:** incomplete as-built records are named as an active risk during renovation `[S1]`.
*   **Decision Gap:** *(unchanged)* Groundskeepers cannot independently diagnose or locate systems in the field, requiring central supervisor dispatch.
*   **Design Opportunity:** *(unchanged)* Spatial Legibility Layer.

> **Domain A is the project's best-evidenced problem** — and it survived the audit strengthened. The "mental maps of microsites" held by long-tenured staff `[S4]`, `[S7]` are corroborated across the corpus.

### Problem Domain B: The Temporal Transition Chokepoint

*   **The Problem:** *(revised)* Volatile climate cycles compress the narrow weather windows available to transition the physical landscape from **growing-season agronomy to winter infrastructure protection** — a pivot in which several decisions are irreversible.
*   **Source Evidence:** *(**evidence base replaced**)* The property pivots from agronomic stewardship to snow-and-frozen-infrastructure logistics on a compressed schedule `[S9]`: the irrigation **"Point of No Return"** blowout must be completed before deep ground freeze or mainlines crack `[S9]`; **Fall Snow Mold fungicide has a ~48-hour application window** before permanent snowpack sets, and missing it risks systemic turf death `[S3]`, `[S9]`; snowpack runs **November through April** `[S8]`; the construction and winterization window is **4–5 months**, and early October snow can end all projects abruptly `[S9]`.
*   **Environmental Dimension:** *(revised)* Systemic turf loss under snowpack if chemical timing is missed; erosion and localized flooding during "The Great Melt" spring runoff when culverts are not manually cleared `[S7]`, `[S9]`.
*   **Operational Dimension:** *(revised)* Simultaneous fleet conversion, chemical application, and infrastructure winterization under one narrow window, with a mechanic reportedly consumed by reactive repair `[S2]`, `[S9]`.
*   **Human Dimension:** *(unchanged)* High stress, long hours, and physical exhaustion for grounds crews during uncoordinated shoulder-season scrambles.
*   **Information Gap:** *(revised)* Disconnection between local microclimate conditions (soil temperature trends, forecast fronts) and the irreversible decision points they govern `[S9]`.
*   **Decision Gap:** *(unchanged)* Transition steps are scheduled based on the calendar rather than real-time terrain conditions.
*   **Design Opportunity:** *(revised per Product Boundary 4)* **Transition Guidance** — surfacing closing windows and readiness by zone for a supervisor to sequence, approve, or override. **Not automated labor routing.**

> **What changed and why — the most significant revision in v3.** v2 built this entire domain on *"10 miles of biking trails into 20 km of groomed Nordic ski trails"* `[U:5, 73]`. That claim is untraceable, and the corpus's actual transition document `[S9]` describes something else entirely — turf, irrigation, snow mold, fleet, and snow logistics, with no trail network. **The domain is not weakened by this correction; it is substantially strengthened.** The replacement evidence supplies something the original never had: *named, irreversible, time-boxed decisions* — a 48-hour chemical window and a point of no return. Those are far better decision-support material than a seasonal trail conversion.

### Problem Domain C: Stewardship Decision-Making Under Changing Conditions

*   **The Problem:** *(unchanged)* The difficulty of making highly contextual stewardship and resource-allocation decisions amidst extreme seasonal volatility without reliable, consolidated operational memory.
*   **Source Evidence:** *(re-sourced v3.1)* Stewardship calls are made on individual judgment against fragmented or unrecorded history `[SRC]`, under conditions that shift faster than the calendar anticipates. Those calls are bounded by real constraints — a fixed irrigation water right `[SRC]`, wildfire-buffer obligations `[SRC]`, and watershed compliance `[SRC]` — which the decision-maker must hold in mind without a consolidated place to see them. It carries wildfire-buffer obligations during the August–early-October dry window `[P4]`, `[S7]`, snowpack loading November–April `[S8]`, and shoreline/watershed compliance exposure including 50-foot setbacks and stormwater BMPs `[S3]`. Turf is maintained as **Dominator Bentgrass** `[S2]`, a high-maintenance cool-season stand on granitic/sandy soils `[S8]`.
*   **Environmental Dimension:** *(revised)* Strain on the Payette Lake basin from uncalibrated irrigation, delayed erosion inspection, or stormwater BMP failure — with **IDEQ intervention as the named regulatory consequence** `[S3]`.
*   **Operational Dimension:** *(revised)* Every irrigated acre is zero-sum against a legal cap `[P1]`; "dry-back" of one area to protect another is an actual, documented trade-off `[S6]`. Calendar-based watering under a fixed ceiling is a compliance risk, not merely an efficiency one.
*   **Human Dimension:** *(unchanged)* Supervisors must make high-stakes operational calls using intuitive, fragmented, or unrecorded historical practices.
*   **Information Gap:** *(unchanged)* Absence of a consolidated layer connecting conditions, historical manual measurements, and qualitative observations.
*   **Decision Gap:** *(unchanged)* Inability to track historical outcomes or cross-reference resource demands against localized observations.
*   **Design Opportunity:** *(unchanged)* The DECISION capability of the Stewardship Intelligence System.

> **What changed and why.** v2 grounded this domain in *"flat-rate, paycheck-deducted housing utility billing"* `[U:2]` and *"102°F summer peaks"* `[U:4]` — both untraceable, and the former already demoted to context in `01-system-planning.md` (Refinement 3). The water-right ceiling replaces them: legally binding, shared across both domains, and genuinely zero-sum.

## 3. System Model

```
 ┌─────────┐     ┌───────────┐     ┌───────────┐     ┌──────────┐
 │  PLACE  ├────►│ CONDITION ├────►│  CONTEXT  ├────►│ DECISION │
 └─────────┘     └───────────┘     └───────────┘     └────┬─────┘
      ▲                                                    │
 ┌────┴──────┐     ┌─────────────┐     ┌───────────┐      │
 │ KNOWLEDGE │◄────┤ OBSERVATION │◄────┤  ACTION   │◄─────┘
 └───────────┘     └─────────────┘     └───────────┘
```

*(Loop closure made explicit in v3 — KNOWLEDGE re-enters as CONTEXT. Node definitions per `01-system-planning.md` §3.2, unchanged.)*

*   **PLACE:** The physical landscape asset, geolocated — e.g. a fairway `[S1]`, a Payette Lake shoreline segment `[S3]`. **Spans both operational domains.**
*   **CONDITION:** The current physical state of that place. Sourced from manual inspection, weather, historical records, GIS layers, qualitative observation, or handheld measurement `[S6]`. **Does not require or assume an automated sensor network.** *(unchanged from v2)*
*   **CONTEXT:** The environmental, seasonal, regulatory, and systemic parameters shaping the condition — a dry cycle, a closing transition window, a known resource limit `[SRC]`, a compound shock.
*   **DECISION:** The operational choice, **with rationale recorded.**
*   **ACTION:** Field execution by crew.
*   **OBSERVATION:** Ground-level recording of the result.
*   **KNOWLEDGE:** Preservation as permanent operational memory, re-entering as CONTEXT.

### Capabilities *(unchanged from v2 in substance; citations re-sourced)*

1.  **PLACE (Spatial Knowledge):** Maps and geolocates critical physical and subterranean infrastructure across both domains — **directly addressing the documented gap in buried-utility as-builts** `[S1]`, `[S4]`.
2.  **CHANGE (Seasonal and Environmental Conditions):** Records and visualizes qualitative and climatic shifts to support the agronomy→winter transition and its irreversible decision points `[S9]`.
3.  **DECISION (Contextual Stewardship Actions):** Cross-references observations against the water-right ceiling `[P1]`, agronomic needs, and regulatory obligations `[S3]`, `[P3]`.
4.  **KNOWLEDGE (Observations & Institutional Memory):** Captures field observations and outcomes, translating brief tenures into permanent organizational memory `[S2]`, `[S4]`.
