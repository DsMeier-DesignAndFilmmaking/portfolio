# Portfolio Homepage Audit
## Step-by-Step Review Against the Strategy

> **How to use this document:** Work through each section in order. Each step includes what to look for, the specific prompt to evaluate against, what the current page actually does, and what needs to change. Complete the scoring for each section at the end.

---

## Pre-Audit Setup

Before starting, open your live homepage and keep `portfolio-homepage-strategy.md` open in a second window. For each section, you're answering one master question:

> *"If someone who doesn't know me lands on this page and spends 90 seconds here, what do they conclude about who I am and what I do?"*

A secondary check runs throughout: *"Does this read as a systems thinker working at the intersection of people, places, and environments — or does it read as a UX/Product Designer?"*

## Section 1 — Hero

**What's currently on the page:**
- H1: *"Designing Experiences Across People, Places, & Systems"*
- Body copy: *"I'm Dan Meier. For 15+ years, I've worked with teams to design, test, and ship digital products, human experiences, and systems architecture. My design and build process started in landscape architecture and urban design..."*
- Secondary line: *"Currently exploring how AI can augment human decision-making in dynamic environments, from digital products to physical places."*
- A handwritten signature image
- A large hero photograph

---

### Step 1.1 — Audit the H1 Title

**Prompt:** Does the headline state what you *do* or what you *make*?

The current H1 — *"Designing Experiences Across People, Places, & Systems"* — is process-framing ("designing") rather than identity-framing ("I am a..."). It's a step away from being a practice title.

**Ask yourself:**
- Does this function as a positioning statement, or as a tagline?
- Would someone immediately know what kind of practitioner you are?
- Does it invite the right audience in (outdoor hospitality, recreation, destination development) or is it generic enough to fit any designer?

**Issue to resolve:** The headline works as a descriptor but doesn't name you as a *practitioner*. Someone skimming won't clock "systems designer" — they'll clock "designer."

**Decision required:** Add your practice title (e.g., *Experience Systems Designer*) either above the H1 as a kicker line, or immediately below it as a subtitle. The H1 can stay as-is if a title line precedes it.

---

### Step 1.2 — Audit the Opening Bio Sentence

**Prompt:** What's the first concrete thing the body copy tells a visitor about you?

Current first sentence: *"For 15+ years, I've worked with teams to design, test, and ship digital products, human experiences, and systems architecture."*

**Issue:** "Design, test, and ship digital products" is pure product design language. It's the first substantive claim you make about yourself, and it positions you as a digital product practitioner. Everything after that corrects it — but you've already sent the wrong signal.

**Ask yourself:**
- Is "ship digital products" the first thing the right client or employer should hear?
- Does "digital products" appear before or after any mention of physical environments, systems, or place?

**Fix direction:** Rewrite the opening sentence to lead with the through-line (people/places/systems), not the deliverable type (digital products). The landscape architecture background should be introduced as a *frame*, not a timeline footnote.

Draft to react to:
> *"I'm Dan Meier — a systems designer who has spent 15 years studying how people move through, interact with, and experience designed environments. First physical, then digital, now the convergence of both."*

---

### Step 1.3 — Audit the "Currently Exploring" Line

**Prompt:** Does this line strengthen or dilute your positioning?

Current line: *"Currently exploring how AI can augment human decision-making in dynamic environments, from digital products to physical places."*

**Issue:** This anchors you to an AI/tech trend rather than to environmental and experiential systems. It signals "designer following the AI wave" rather than "systems thinker applied to specific domains."

**Ask yourself:**
- Is AI augmentation the core of your practice direction, or a tool/method within it?
- Does this line make you more differentiated or more similar to thousands of other designers right now?
- Would an outdoor hospitality operator, recreation organization, or destination developer read this and feel like it speaks to their world?

**Fix direction:** Replace or reframe this line around the actual domain direction. Something that names outdoor, environmental, or experiential systems as the terrain — and positions AI (if relevant) as one lens within that, not the headline.

---

### Step 1.4 — Audit the Hero Image

**Prompt:** What does the hero image communicate before a word is read?

Current: A full-width photo of Dan Meier (person portrait).

**Ask yourself:**
- Does a portrait photo communicate *practice* or *person*?
- Is this image doing strategic work, or is it a placeholder for "there should be something big here"?
- What would a visitor who doesn't scroll past this image understand about your practice?

**Note:** A portrait isn't wrong — but as a *hero image* it communicates personality before it communicates practice. Consider whether environmental photography (a place you've documented, a landscape you've studied, something from your 41-country archive) would do more positioning work here.

## Section 2 — About / "The Lens Behind the Work"

**What's currently on the page:**
- H2: *"The Lens Behind the Work"*
- Sub-label: *"From Place to Product"*
- Two portrait photos, followed by copy
- Copy: mentions landscape architecture → UX/product design → travel as three connected threads
- The experience concept sketch (People / Places / Systems / Experiences diagram)
- Four stacked portrait photos total
- Travel copy mentioning 41 countries

---

### Step 2.1 — Audit the Section Title and Sub-label

**Prompt:** Does *"The Lens Behind the Work"* + *"From Place to Product"* tell the right story?

**Issue with "From Place to Product":** This is a transition narrative. It implies landscape architecture was the origin and "product" (digital) is where you arrived. That's a career-change framing, not a continuous-practice framing. It inadvertently frames the landscape architecture background as the "before" rather than as a permanent lens that still operates.

**Ask yourself:**
- Does "Product" describe where you want to be going? Or is it describing where you've been?
- What would someone conclude about your direction if they only read this label?

**Fix direction:** Replace the sub-label with something that communicates the *synthesis* rather than the journey. Options to react to:
- *"A practice built across two environments"*
- *"15 years across physical and digital systems"*
- *"Where landscape thinking meets systems design"*

---

### Step 2.2 — Audit the Photo Count

**Prompt:** Do four portrait photos earn their place on the page?

**Current:** Four full-width portrait photos are stacked in the About section.

**Issue:** Multiple portrait photos is a fashion/lifestyle convention, not a practitioner convention. It allocates real estate to personality at the expense of practice. On a portfolio that's arguing for strategic depth, this ratio is off.

**Ask yourself:**
- What does the fourth portrait add that the first doesn't?
- Could this space be used for something that demonstrates how you *think* rather than who you *look like*?
- Would a research artifact, environmental photo, or field documentation image do more positioning work here?

**Fix direction:** Keep one strong portrait. Replace the others with visual artifacts that demonstrate the practice — field photography, sketches, research documentation, environmental observation.

---

### Step 2.3 — Audit the About Narrative Copy

**Prompt:** Read the copy out loud. Does it describe a continuous practice or a career change?

**Current copy framing:** Landscape architecture → UX/product design → travel observation. Three separate threads held together by the shared word "design."

**Ask yourself:**
- Is the word "systems" used to connect these threads, or does each period stand alone?
- Is landscape architecture framed as something you *used to do*, or as a lens you still carry?
- Does the copy explain *why* the combination produces something no single-domain practitioner can offer?

**Issue:** The "grit of manual labor" mention is authentic but potentially distracting. It reads as a personality detail rather than a practice credential.

**The about copy needs to do one job:** Explain why 15 years across physical and digital environments makes you *more* capable of understanding complex human-environment systems — not just *different*.

---

### Step 2.4 — Audit the Experience Concept Sketch

**Prompt:** Is this diagram earning its place?

**Current:** A hand-drawn SVG sketch showing People / Places / Systems / Experiences as interconnected nodes with sub-annotations.

**Verdict: Keep it.** This is genuinely differentiated. A hand-drawn systems diagram is unusual, signals original thinking, and visually reinforces the practice positioning in a way text can't. It's one of the stronger elements on the page.

**One refinement check:**
- Does the diagram have any explanatory framing around it (a sentence before or after that tells the reader *what they're looking at* and *why it matters*)?
- If it stands alone without context, consider a 1–2 sentence frame: what does this diagram represent, and why is it the organizing logic of your practice?

## Section 3 — Work (DesignWork Component)

**What's currently on the page:**
- Section labeled "Work"
- H1: *"Designing confidence across products, services, and environments."*
- Subtitle: *"Selected work exploring how people navigate complexity across products, services, and environments."*
- Four categories: Research → Frameworks → Concepts → Builds & Implementation
- **Research:** 1 card — Environmental Systems Design OS
- **Frameworks:** 1 card — Architecture of Confidence
- **Concepts:** (need to confirm, likely placeholder or sparse)
- **Builds:** (professional practice section + linked cards)

---

### Step 3.1 — Audit the Work Section Heading

**Prompt:** Does *"Designing confidence across products, services, and environments"* communicate what you do or describe one project?

**Issue:** "Designing confidence" is the title of your *Architecture of Confidence* framework. Using it as the Work section headline makes the entire section feel like one project rather than a breadth of practice.

**Ask yourself:**
- Does someone reading this headline understand the scope of your practice?
- Would someone unfamiliar with your work understand that "confidence" here is a design philosophy, not a vague aspiration?

**Fix direction:** The Work section heading should describe the *practice structure*, not borrow language from one framework. Options:
- *"Work organized around understanding systems deeply"*
- *"Research. Frameworks. Concepts. Builds."* (let the structure speak)
- *"A practice built to understand systems before improving them"*

---

### Step 3.2 — Audit the Four-Category Structure

**Prompt:** Does the Research → Frameworks → Concepts → Builds structure read clearly to a first-time visitor?

**What to check:**
- Are the four category names visible as distinct navigational anchors, or do they blend into the card flow?
- Is there any brief framing text explaining what each category *means* before the cards appear?
- Would someone who isn't a designer immediately understand why Research comes before Builds?

**Fix direction:** Add a single-sentence descriptor beneath each category name — not a label, but a purpose statement. Example:

> **Research** — *Observation, pattern identification, and systems understanding from real environments*

> **Frameworks** — *Original models that simplify complexity and transfer across industries*

> **Concepts** — *Applied ideas for places, experiences, and operations*

> **Builds & Implementation** — *Delivered systems and professional practice work*

This takes 4 lines and makes the intellectual logic of the structure legible to anyone.

---

### Step 3.3 — Audit Work Volume and Balance

**Prompt:** Is there enough work in each category to make the structure feel like a real practice, or does it feel like scaffolding?

**Current state:**
- Research: 1 item
- Frameworks: 1 item
- Concepts: likely sparse
- Builds: professional practice + linked cards

**Issue:** One item per category, especially in Research and Frameworks, makes the structure feel aspirational rather than substantive. A visitor could read the whole Work section in under two minutes and come away with no strong impression of practice depth.

**Ask yourself:**
- Which categories are genuinely thin vs. which have more work that just hasn't been added yet?
- What's the minimum viable content per category that would feel like a real body of work? (Recommendation: 2–3 items per category at launch)
- Are there research studies, frameworks, or concepts in your existing work (even rough or in-progress) that could be surfaced here?

---

### Step 3.4 — Audit the "Architecture of Confidence" Framework Card

**Prompt:** Does the card description communicate the framework's power in 2–3 sentences?

**Current description:** *"A transferable framework for designing decision support that reduces uncertainty, preserves agency, and helps people recover when conditions change."*

**Verdict: Strong.** This is clear, domain-spanning language that doesn't reduce to UX jargon. The words "transferable," "uncertainty," "agency," and "recovery" all carry systems-thinking weight.

**One check:** Do the tags (*Decision support, Confidence, Recovery*) signal the breadth of where this framework applies? A visitor working in outdoor hospitality, recreation, or destination development might not immediately see their domain in those tags. Consider whether adding 1–2 domain tags (e.g., *Wayfinding, Visitor experience*) would help them self-identify as relevant.

---

### Step 3.5 — Audit the Builds Section

**Prompt:** Does the Builds section feel like proof of execution, or like a list of old jobs?

**What to check without seeing every card:**
- Are the build cards described in terms of *what system was built and what it achieved*, or in terms of *client name and deliverable type*?
- Does each card connect to the broader practice language (systems, environments, experience) or does it revert to "I designed the UX for..."?
- Is there any risk that the Builds section reads as a conventional UX portfolio that contradicts the positioning of everything above it?

**Fix direction:** Every build card should have a one-sentence *systems framing* — what system was the work part of, and what did it change? Even for digital work, the framing can be: *"Redesigned the operational onboarding system for X, reducing decision fatigue for new users navigating..."*

## Section 4 — Travelogue / "Field Notes From the World"

**What's currently on the page:**
- Section heading: *"Field Notes From the World"*
- Explanation copy about travel as observation practice
- Vimeo video embed (full-width)
- Six image grid of video still frames from various countries
- YouTube and Vimeo links
- "Travel Field Journal" card — marked as "Developing"
- "Field journal in progress" note at the bottom

---

### Step 4.1 — Audit the Section's Strategic Purpose

**Prompt:** Does this section *prove something about your practice* or does it demonstrate a personal interest?

**Issue:** Travel is one of your strongest differentiators — 41 countries, personal photography, real-world observation. That's legitimate practice credibility. But the section currently reads as a travel page, not a systems practice page. The connection between "I traveled here" and "this shaped how I understand human-environment systems" is implied, not stated.

**Ask yourself:**
- Would a hiring manager at a destination development company or a recreation organization read this section and conclude "this person observes environments professionally"?
- Or would they conclude "this person travels a lot and documents it nicely"?

**Fix direction:** The framing copy needs to explicitly connect travel observation to *systems practice*. One specific claim is stronger than a general one. Example: instead of "Travel deepened that lens," name *what specific thing* you learned to see — wayfinding under uncertainty, hospitality friction, how signage fails visitors in unfamiliar environments, how informal systems emerge when formal ones fail.

---

### Step 4.2 — Audit the Section's Proportional Weight

**Prompt:** Does the Travelogue section's length and prominence match its role in your positioning?

**Current state:** This is a long section — full-width video, six image grids, a call-to-action card, a tech stack note. It is likely the longest section on the page.

**Issue:** Travel observation is supporting evidence for your practice. It should be compelling but not dominant. Currently it may receive more visual weight than the Work section — which is where your practice lives.

**Ask yourself:**
- Does a first-time visitor spend more time in the Travelogue than in the Work section?
- Does this section feel proportional to its role as contextual evidence (not a primary credential)?
- The "Travel Field Journal — Developing" card promises something that doesn't exist yet. Does an unfulfilled promise weaken the page?

**Fix direction:** Consider whether this section can be condensed — one strong image, the core framing paragraph, and a link to a future field journal page — rather than a full portfolio section. The existing content can become the foundation of a standalone page.

---

### Step 4.3 — Audit the "Developing Field Journal" Promise

**Prompt:** Does a "coming soon" card strengthen or weaken the page?

**Current:** A prominent card labeled *"Developing Field Journal"* with a clock icon.

**Issue:** Unfulfilled promises on a portfolio page signal incompleteness. A visitor who sees "Developing" may apply that perception to the whole site — "this is a work in progress."

**Decision required:**
- Remove the card until the journal exists, or
- Replace "Developing" with a brief, specific description of what the journal will contain and why it will be different from standard travel photography

### Step 5.1 — Audit for a Contact Entry Point

**Prompt:** Can someone who finishes reading the homepage take an obvious next step?

**Issue:** There is no contact section, no collaboration framing, no email or form visible on the homepage. A motivated visitor reaches the bottom of the page with no clear action to take.

**This is a significant gap.** Every portfolio with positioning ambitions needs a low-friction contact/collaboration entry point. ## Section 6 — Overall Positioning Coherence

Run these checks across the full page as a whole, not section by section.

---

### Step 6.1 — The 5-Second Test

**Prompt:** Look at only the top of the page — hero title, subtitle, and opening paragraph. Without scrolling, what practice does this communicate?

Write down what a stranger would conclude. Compare it to: *"Systems designer working at the intersection of people, places, environments, and digital systems."*

If there's a gap, the hero section needs work.

---

### Step 6.2 — The Language Audit

**Prompt:** Search the page for these terms. Count how many times each appears.

| Term | Count | Target |
|---|---|---|
| "digital products" or "product design" | | Minimize — max 1 |
| "UX" or "UI" | | Minimize — max 1 |
| "systems" | | Should appear frequently |
| "environment" or "environmental" | | Should appear several times |
| "experience" (in context of place/service) | | Should appear |
| "people, places" or equivalent | | Should appear in hero or near-hero |

If "digital products" outweighs "systems" or "environments" in raw count, the language hasn't caught up to the strategy.

---

### Step 6.3 — The Audience Test

**Prompt:** Read the page as if you are the director of experience design at a national park concessionaire, a resort group, or a destination development organization. 

Ask:
- Do I see myself and my problems reflected here?
- Would I feel like this person understands my world — the operational complexity, the environmental context, the visitor journey?
- Is there enough specificity about physical environments and real-world systems that I'd trust this person to work on my kind of problem?

If the answer is "not clearly," identify the specific moments where the page loses that audience.

---

### Step 6.4 — The Anti-Pattern Check

Check whether any of the following appear on the page. Each is a positioning leak:

- [ ] A list of software tools (Figma, Sketch, etc.) anywhere on the homepage
- [ ] "User-centric" or "human-centered design" language
- [ ] A timeline or chronological career structure
- [ ] Any section that reads as a resume rather than a practice description
- [ ] Work described primarily by client name rather than system or problem
- [ ] Any mention of "passion" as a substitute for demonstrated expertise
- [ ] A section that would make a conventional UX recruiter feel at home but make a destination developer confused

---

### Step 6.5 — The Scalability Check

**Prompt:** If you added "Studio" after your name tomorrow, what would break?

- Does any language on the page *only* work for an individual (e.g., "I am a designer who...")?
- Is there room for collaborators, client engagements, or a service description without rebuilding the page?
- Does the brand language work for a one-person practice *and* a 3-person studio?

Note anything that would require a rewrite, not just a small edit.

- [ ] Add a contact/collaboration section to the homepage footer
- [ ] Add purpose statements (one sentence each) beneath Research / Frameworks / Concepts / Builds category headers

### Tier 2 — Improve Within 30 Days

- [ ] Reduce About section portrait photos from 4 to 1 — use the freed space for practice artifacts
- [ ] Rewrite Work section headline — "Designing confidence..." borrows too specifically from one framework
- [ ] Add at minimum 1 more Research item and 1 more Concept item to the Work section
- [ ] Add explicit systems framing to every Build card description
- [ ] Tighten the Travelogue section — cut it to one strong image, core framing paragraph, and a link

## Prompts to Use When Rewriting

These are direct prompts you can use in this chat session to generate improved copy for specific sections:

**Hero opening:**
> "Rewrite the hero bio opening for my portfolio. Current text: [paste text]. It should lead with systems and environments, not digital products. The through-line is 15 years across physical and digital environments, from landscape architecture to digital systems to convergence."

**About sub-label:**
> "Generate 5 options for a 3–6 word sub-label for my About section that communicates a continuous, evolving practice across physical and digital environments — not a career transition from landscape architecture to product design."

**Work section headline:**
> "Write 5 options for a Work section headline that describes a systems design practice spanning environmental observation, framework development, concepts, and professional execution. Should not borrow language from any specific project."

**Category purpose statements:**
> "Write one-sentence purpose statements for each of these portfolio categories as they apply to an environmental and experiential systems design practice: Research, Frameworks, Concepts, Builds & Implementation."

**Contact framing:**
> "Write a 3-sentence contact/collaboration section for my portfolio homepage. I'm interested in working with outdoor hospitality operators, recreation organizations, public land managers, destination developers, and others working at the intersection of place and human experience."

**Travelogue section framing:**
> "Rewrite this travel section intro to explicitly connect field observation across 41 countries to systems design practice — specifically to how I observe wayfinding, hospitality, environmental complexity, and visitor behavior. Should feel like professional field research, not personal travel."
