# Application Strategy

## The Core Rule
**No mass applying.**

Every application that is not tailored is a wasted shot and a burned impression. If the role is worth applying to, it is worth 15 minutes of tailoring. If it is not worth 15 minutes, do not apply.

---

## Application Qualification Filter

Before applying, answer these three questions:

1. **Does this company build products where systems thinking is valued?** (Not just UI execution)
2. **Is there a connection between what I've built and the problem they're solving?** (HADE, travel context, AI-native UX, trust layers)
3. **Can I say something specific about their product that shows I've thought about it?** (Not generic praise — a real observation)

If the answer to any of these is no, skip the application.

---

## Tailoring Framework

### Step 1: Read the job description for the actual product problem
Don't read it for the keyword list. Read it for the product thinking behind it.

Look for:
- What is the hard product challenge they haven't solved yet?
- What domain complexity are they hiring someone to navigate?
- What does "success" look like in the first 6 months?

### Step 2: Find the overlap with HADE or your work
Map their challenge to your system:

| Their Problem | Your Evidence |
|---------------|--------------|
| Context-aware personalization | HADE context engine + adaptation loop |
| AI trust and transparency | Logic Receipt design + ZK-proof layer |
| Ambient recommendation UX | Detour suggestion redesign (modal → overlay) |
| Social graph features | Social Proximity Module + Dopamine Governor |
| Travel or mobility domain | Full HADE system + CATDS |

### Step 3: Write one tailored paragraph
This is the only customization required. One paragraph that connects their specific challenge to your specific system.

**Template:**
> "At [Company], you're working on [specific problem from JD]. I've been building directly in this space — [HADE / specific project] addresses [the same structural challenge] by [the specific mechanism]. The part I found most interesting to solve was [constraint or decision that shows depth]. I'd like to talk about how that thinking applies to [their product]."

### Step 4: Submit with that paragraph as the cover or application note
If there is no cover letter field, put it in the portfolio link note, the intro message, or the LinkedIn message accompanying the application.

---

## Aligning HADE to Company Product Thinking

HADE is the anchor. The goal is to make every company see HADE as relevant to their specific product challenge.

### For AI-native startups:
Lead with the trust layer and adaptation loop. These companies care about AI product design that goes beyond the surface.

### For travel companies (Airbnb, Booking, Tripadvisor):
Lead with the domain depth — you understand the specific human problems in travel (detour anxiety, decision fatigue, social friction) and have built system-level responses to them.

### For mobility and location-intelligence platforms:
Lead with the context engine — real-time telemetry, environmental sensing, latency constraints.

### For design systems or platform teams:
Lead with the transferability angle — HADE's architecture is a pattern that applies beyond travel. The trust layer, suggestion engine, and adaptation loop are reusable system primitives.

---

## The Goal of Every Application

The hiring manager should finish reading your application thinking:

> "This person already thinks like us."

Not: "This person could learn to think like us."
Not: "This person has relevant experience."
Not: "This person seems smart."

**This person already thinks like us.**

That requires specificity. Generic applications cannot achieve this.

---

## Application Tracking

Track every application with:
- Company name
- Role
- The specific problem you identified from the JD
- The HADE/project angle you used
- Date submitted
- Status

This prevents repeat outreach to the same company and builds a record of what angles resonate.
