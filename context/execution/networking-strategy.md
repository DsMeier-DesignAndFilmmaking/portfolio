# Networking Strategy

## The Core Rule
**Conversations, not job asking.**

The goal of every outreach message is to start a conversation. Not to get a referral. Not to ask about openings. Not to pitch yourself. A conversation.

Job opportunities come from relationships. Relationships come from genuine exchanges. Genuine exchanges come from asking good questions about things the other person actually cares about.

---

## Who to Reach Out To

### Tier 1: Direct Decision-Makers (highest value)
- **Product Design Managers** at target companies (will be the hiring manager)
- **Head of Product Design** at AI-native startups (often makes the call directly)
- **Product Managers** working on travel or AI products (can refer internally)

### Tier 2: Peers and Adjacent Roles (medium value)
- **Senior Product Designers** at target companies (can give inside perspective, may refer)
- **Design System leads** (often know about hiring before it's posted)
- **AI product managers** at travel companies (understands the domain, may champion)

### Tier 3: Community and Amplifiers (slow build, high long-term value)
- Writers and thinkers publishing on AI-native product design
- Designers posting systems work publicly (X/Twitter, LinkedIn, are.na)
- Conference speakers at travel tech or AI design events

---

## Daily Outreach Targets

- **2–3 messages per day**
- Never more than 3 — quality degrades fast after that
- Prioritize Tier 1 when actively job searching
- Maintain 1 Tier 3 message per day for long-term relationship building

---

## Message Templates

### Cold Outreach — Specific Product Observation
Use when: You noticed something specific about their product or work that you can speak to genuinely.

```
Hi [Name],

I've been working on AI-native travel systems and came across your work on [specific feature/post/talk].

[One specific observation that shows you actually engaged with their work — not generic praise.]

Curious about [one specific question that shows systems-level thinking].

Happy to share what I've been building if there's any interest in comparing notes.

— Daniel
```

---

### Warm Outreach — Shared Domain or Interest
Use when: You have a real connection point (mutual contact, same domain, commented on their work before).

```
Hi [Name],

[Reference the connection point — brief, direct.]

I've been building in the same space — specifically [HADE / travel AI / trust layers]. Your work on [their specific area] overlaps with something I solved differently.

Would you be up for a 20-minute call to compare approaches?

— Daniel
```

---

### Follow-Up After Application
Use when: You've applied to a role and want to create a warm signal before the interview stage.

```
Hi [Name],

I applied for the [Role] role at [Company] last week.

I've been building [HADE / relevant project] — specifically the [component that overlaps with their product challenge]. Thought it might be relevant context given what [Company] is working on.

Happy to share more if useful.

— Daniel
```

---

### Response to Their Published Work
Use when: They published something (article, case study, tweet thread) that you genuinely engaged with.

```
Hi [Name],

Read your [article/thread] on [topic]. The point about [specific insight] was the clearest articulation I've seen of [that specific problem].

I hit a similar constraint building [HADE component]. Ended up solving it by [one sentence on your approach]. Curious if you went a different direction.

— Daniel
```

---

## Rules for Every Message

- **Max 5 sentences.** If you can't say it in 5 sentences, you haven't thought it through.
- **One specific observation.** Generic compliments are worse than silence.
- **One question.** Not a list of questions. One.
- **No job mention in the first message.** Not even "I'm open to opportunities."
- **No portfolio link unless asked or the message is explicitly about the work.**

---

## Follow-Up Cadence

- If no response after 7 days: one follow-up, one sentence, same thread
- If no response after follow-up: move on — do not send a third message
- If they respond: reply within 24 hours, keep the conversation going, be useful

---

## What "Being Useful" Looks Like

- Share something relevant to their work (article, tool, observation) — not to impress, but because it's genuinely useful
- Ask a question that shows you've thought about their product challenge
- Offer a perspective from your own work that's relevant to a problem they mentioned
- Make an introduction if you know someone who'd be valuable to them

The best networkers are the ones who make other people smarter. Be that person.
